# Executive Readiness Summary — EU AI Act
**Lumina Mobility B.V.** · Prepared for: Board of Directors · Date: 2026-10-08 · Classification: Board Confidential

---

## The Bottom Line

> **Lumina is in scope of the EU AI Act as a *provider* of two high-risk AI systems — the Dispatch Optimisation Engine and the Driver Performance Score — because both make or shape decisions about drivers' work. We are at an *Initial* level of readiness (1.4 out of 4). Full obligations apply from 2 December 2027. Reaching compliance requires a 14-month programme of roughly 2,950 person-days, estimated at €2.1–2.6m. Three obligations already apply today and need action this quarter.**

## 1. What We Assessed
Eight AI systems across Lumina Fleet (B2B SaaS) and Lumina Ride (own app), against the EU AI Act as amended by the Digital Omnibus (Regulation (EU) 2026/1744).

## 4. The Three Issues the Board Should Know About

**1. Our scoring may be treating some drivers unfairly — and we haven't checked.**
The Driver Performance Score relies partly on rider ratings, which research shows can reflect rider bias. Lower-scored drivers then receive ~18% lower-value trips from the dispatch engine. This compounding effect has never been tested. *This is both the largest legal exposure and a reputational and driver-retention risk.*

**2. Customers can turn our product into an automated firing tool.**
Fleet operators can configure the score to deactivate drivers automatically. Under the AI Act (and the Platform Work Directive, which member states must transpose by 2 December 2026), significant decisions about workers need meaningful human oversight. We recommend removing this capability now.

**3. Some obligations are already live.**
AI literacy training, the ban on emotion recognition at work (a planned "driver mood" feature has been stopped), and disclosure that users are talking to an AI apply today.

## 5. Strategic Options Considered

| Option | Description | Assessment |
|---|---|---|
| A. Full compliance programme (recommended) | Remediate S1, S2, S4 to meet Arts. 8–17 and conformity requirements | Protects EU revenue (~82% of total); turns compliance into a sales differentiator for fleet customers who need it for their own Art. 26 duties |
| B. Re-engineer out of scope | Remove behavioural features from dispatch | Possible for S1 only; S2 remains high-risk; −4–6% matching efficiency; saves ~25% of effort. Not recommended alone |
| C. Defer | Wait for harmonised standards | Leaves already-applicable gaps open; compresses 14 months of work; high risk of missing deadline |
